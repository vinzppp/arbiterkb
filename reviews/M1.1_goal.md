**My one sharp objection: AURC against a binary haircut favours ArbiterKB by construction, and spec v1.3 builds that bias into M2 (c).** OverlapHaircut and MarginHaircut output only two confidence values (1 or c), so all flagged rows tie. ArbiterKB outputs a graded score that is a monotone function of the same feature. Any graded score wins the AURC tie-break, with or without a belief layer.

## Claim movement
- **Claim 1: none.** The corruption is still ours.
- **Where ArbiterKB's AURC win comes from.** It reports 0.011 vs OverlapHaircut's 0.020 under id_swap_overlap (`results/latest/m1_1_table/summary.csv`). In `results/runs/synthetic_test_2f707774/rows.csv`:
  - ArbiterKB's prediction equals Bound's in **120/120** rows (clean and id_swap_overlap). The belief layer never changed an answer.
  - Its only effect is the confidence. OverlapHaircut ties 12 rows (6 wrong, 6 right) at 0.5. ArbiterKB spreads those 12 rows over 0.495–0.815, with 5 of the 6 wrong rows at the bottom of that range.
  - That ranking is just R6 v1.5's w = 0.5·exp(−d/d0) applied to the minimum 3D distance. So the AURC gap measures a graded score against a binary one on the same feature. It is not evidence for a belief layer.
- **Falsifiers and claim 2:** not touched. The "AUC ≥ 0.98" gate is correctly placed in G.

## Risks of self-deception
1. **The AURC win is structural (the main objection).**
   - M2 (c) asks for "AURC lower" than OverlapHaircut and MarginHaircut, both of which are binary. ArbiterKB will pass that test whatever the belief layer does.
   - **Cheapest control:** add `SoftHaircut(feature)`. It is Bound's answer with confidence 1 − w_max·exp(−feature/scale), the same formula R6 uses, with no KB. Run it on distance now and on the margin in M2.
   - Today, SoftHaircut(min 3D distance) should match ArbiterKB's AURC almost exactly. Confirm that in about 30 minutes on the existing rows plus the `corruption_log` in meta.json.
   - ArbiterKB minus SoftHaircut is the only column that isolates propagation (R5, retraction, arbiter). Make it the headline column of M2 (c).
2. **"Matched clean coverage" is not implemented yet.**
   - The table compares OverlapHaircut at coverage 0.80 with ArbiterKB at 0.90. That is not matched.
   - **Control:** sweep the haircut threshold (d_ov, or the margin threshold) on train seeds and read off its silent rate at ArbiterKB's clean coverage.
3. **n is tiny.** The AURC numbers rest on 6 wrong rows.
   - **Control:** in M2, use n ≥ 300 fresh seeds (≥ 1060) and bootstrap CIs for AURC. Wilson intervals do not cover AURC.
4. **Wording:** the notebook and NOTES call OverlapHaircut "Bayes-optimal".
   - That is true only for the hard label at c = 0.5. It is not optimal for ranking, and the AURC result itself shows this. Fix the word so a future reader does not think ArbiterKB beat a Bayes-optimal rule.

## Missing baseline(s)

  - The "best single feature AUC ≥ 0.98" gate can understate how well a trivial rule separates swaps.
