## Claim movement
- **Claim 1 (silent failure): none.** Source: `results/latest/synthetic_test_n30_p1.0/summary.csv`.
  - On the 20 corrupted episodes, ArbiterKB is silent on 0.90 (18/20) and Bound on 1.00 (20/20). The Wilson intervals overlap.
  - ArbiterKB's confidence when wrong is 0.91.
  - At p = 0.5 only 5 episodes are corrupted (0.8 vs 1.0).
  - The corruption is the teleport, so this table cannot count toward the claim at any size.
- **Clean cost (part of claim 1):** accuracy is 1.00 for both systems. ArbiterKB's confidence when right is 0.94 (ECE 0.059). It is measured correctly but nothing has been tuned.
- **Falsifiers, and claim 2:** not touched. That is expected for M0.

## Risks of self-deception
1. **M1's `id_swap_overlap` is still true by construction, now through distance instead of the jump.**
   - **What M1 specifies:**
     - The corruption swaps only when the 3D centre distance d < `d_ov`, with p_swap chosen by us (0.5).
     - R6 v1.5 sets w = w_max·exp(−d/d0) from the same d.
     - `tune.py` fits w_max and d0 on that same corruption.
   - **Why that decides the result in advance:**
     - The swap happens with no jump, by design, so the observations carry no signal that separates swapped from unswapped overlapping passes.
     - The best any system can do is copy our two choices: a step at `d_ov` and the rate p.
     - ArbiterKB will "beat" BoundHaircut, which uses the image near-pass and so also haircuts wide clean passes, only by learning our `d_ov`.
     - The tuned calibration will reproduce p = 0.5.
     - The planned trade-off curve (clean ECE vs silent failure) can be worked out on paper from p and the fraction of overlapping passes. With τ_hi = 0.8, any haircut that pushes confidence below 0.8 after an overlapping pass gives zero silent failure.
   - **Cheapest control:**
     - Add `OverlapHaircut(c)`: Bound, but its answer drops to c only after a pass with min 3D distance < `d_ov`. This is the Bayes-optimal rule under `id_swap_overlap`, about 10 lines.
     - Report ArbiterKB minus OverlapHaircut. If the gap is about 0, M1 has shown that the plumbing works, and nothing about claim 1.
     - Better still, take the dependence of swaps on distance from G, not from `d_ov`.
2. **Provenance is weak (minor).**
   - The committed `results/latest/synthetic_test_n30_p1.0/meta.json` records commit 23b53ba but was produced by the uncommitted M0.1 tree, 3 s before the commit.
   - The p = 0.5 meta records `"commit": "no-git"`.
   - The working tree now has an uncommitted re-run of the same file (commit 12873b8; the summary is unchanged).
   - `results/latest/synthetic_test_n10_p1.0/` is untracked and nothing refers to it.
   - Control: record `git status --porcelain` (a dirty flag) in meta, and delete or ignore the n10 directory.

## Missing baseline(s)
- **OverlapHaircut** (above) is the strongest trivial baseline for M1, and it is stronger than BoundHaircut.
- JumpDetector stays mandatory. By construction it should be useless on `id_swap_overlap`. Confirm that its jump histogram stays at ≤ `d_ov`, i.e. done-when (e).

## Cut or defer
- **Defer from M1 build step 4:** R3 held and the full visibility state machine (`held`, `occluded_by_arm`, `out_of_view`). On VideoUnmaskSwap the cube stays inside a container until t*, and no metric for claim 1 reads these states.
- **Keep in M1:** R5 (`joint_exclusive`). With 3 cubes in 3 containers it is the one rule where ArbiterKB could beat any haircut. Pin the expected gain with the done-when (f) test.
- **Carried over and still unused:** the planner/skills waypoints, `validate()`, the Trigger and RuleOnlyBackend stubs, and R7 at λ = 1. They are cheap; don't extend them.

## Recommendation for the next phase
**Modify: shrink M1 and move G up.** M1 on synthetic `id_swap_overlap` can't decide claim 1, because its answer is set by `d_ov` and p, which we choose.
- **M1 plumbing only:** the overlap generator, BoundHaircut, OverlapHaircut, JumpDetector and R5. Report a test table at untuned defaults plus the jump histogram.
- **Drop from M1:** the `tune.py` grid and the trade-off curve until G's measured signature exists.
- **Then:** spend the saved day writing G's Mac-side code (`stress_env.py`, `measure_swaps.py`) and ask the user for the pod session.
- **Why G decides more:**
  - It is the only planned experiment that can trigger the falsifier "deterministic memory is rarely confidently wrong".
  - It tells us whether real swaps carry a usable signal (`track_q` drop, mask IoU, jump), which is the only way ArbiterKB can beat a haircut.
  - If swaps occur in < 2% of passes in every setting, the M1 tuning would have been wasted work.
